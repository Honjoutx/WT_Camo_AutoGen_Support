Vehicle livery workflow and execution guide

Scope
This guide defines a reusable process. The current project brief controls themes, characters, colors, fonts, orientation and poster style. Do not inherit these choices from other projects.
The workflow screen does not list historical projects, character settings, palettes, unfinished work or output paths. Read historical material only when the user explicitly requests it.

01 Initial model repair and grouping
Inspect OBJ / MTL, geometry, normals, materials, texture paths, UV ranges and repeated sampling. Preserve original files and UV evidence.
For UVs outside 0..1, distinguish integer phase shifts, tiling and mirroring. Repair only proven shifts; never apply per-vertex modulo, repack UVs, weld or change topology without authorization.
Group by structure: hull, turret, barrel, equipment and detachable armor. Tracks may be excluded from painting and restored for rendering. Names and object indices are clues; geometry, materials and assembly determine ownership.
Save repaired models, material relationships, module assignments, uncovered parts and a repair report.

02 Brief and assets
Record theme, palette, characters, fonts, exact text, target surfaces, orientation, cropping, materials, resolution and deliverables.
Preserve source artwork and its provenance. Background removal must not modify a character. Create separate projects for different vehicles or character variants; related projects may share an asset library.

03 Design sketch
Save a viewable sketch before detailed design. Establish large color areas, flow, contrast, density, focal points and character safe zones. Keep the sketch revision.
Check continuity across assembled modules; detail cannot replace the overall composition.

04 Principal physical surfaces
Use large hull and turret sides, tops and front surfaces, plus the barrel and equipment.
A directional region covers the principal structure, not every face visible from that direction. Merge contiguous coplanar plates. Treat slopes and protrusions according to their geometry.
Record actual face IDs, footprints, art coordinates and masks. Place characters on continuous surfaces where protrusions will not split their image.

05 Surface design
Compose each surface independently and check continuity after assembly. Establish the framework, main patterns, detail, characters and typography in that order.
Dense patterns still require hierarchy. Prefer vectors for fine lines, dots, grids, type and color areas; avoid blurry edges and uniform visual noise.
Keep source image quality and alpha. Follow the brief for character direction, scale and crop. Preserve spelling and required fonts.

06 Materials and normals
Distinguish paint, graphics, lettering, metal, rubber, cloth and glass. Set metallic, roughness, relief and shadows by layer and physical material.
Use bright highlights only where the brief calls for them. Smooth fine normal noise while preserving seams, bolts, engraved lines and hard edges.
Check reflections, highlights, exposure and noise in an actual render.

07 Migration between related models
Transfer layouts using physical coordinates and common anchors. Keep each model's original UVs. Crop absent geometry rather than squeezing the full design.
Inventory different equipment. Declare common patterns and priority for shared UVs. Inspect complete source models instead of assuming all base parts match.

08 Mapping inspection and repair
Trace a reported physical part through face IDs, original UVs, ownership, art coordinates, masks, materials, atlas merging and final files.
Distinguish canvas, model-preview, exported-atlas and in-game loading errors. Establish evidence, then change only the affected domain. Report unresolved issues accurately.

09 Export and storage
Preserve original UVs. Supply at least 2K or the requested 4K / 8K resolution. Export only changed original atlases and selected channels, flat in one directory.
Use the established filename rules and Basecolor, Metallic, Roughness, Smoothness, Normal and Height suffixes. Handle game-specific packing separately.
Default to compact storage: reuse immutable assets and create ZIPs when downloaded. Use portable copies for independent handoff. Do not delete shared assets that projects still reference. Rebuild missing caches from valid inputs.

10 Rendering and poster assets
Produce a clean render first. Preserve manually adjusted models and cameras. Follow the current brief for scene, composition, lighting, transparency and background style.
Keep requested floor and contact shadows; denoise adequately. Deliver the model render, character artwork, background, lettering and border separately when requested. Generated images must not distort the finished vehicle render.

Execution and live sync
Prefer background processing and use capabilities actually configured on this machine. Keep inputs, operations, parameters, outputs and necessary evidence. Do not publish automatically.
The interface watches the current layout, sources, masks, assets and configuration. Unsaved manual edits or revision conflicts pause replacement. Back up local edits before loading a background revision.
